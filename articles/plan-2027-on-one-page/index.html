Plan 2027 on One Page in 20 Minutes

A simple year-at-a-glance routine that catches clashes before they happen

DigitalShelf Labs · 22 October 2026

Most planning goes wrong at the week level. You open your calendar on a Monday and discover the work deadline, the family trip and the friend's wedding all landed in the same fortnight. Nobody planned that. It just happened, because nobody ever looked at the whole year at once.

A one-page year view fixes that. It isn't a replacement for your calendar app. It's a twenty-minute exercise you do once, now, before 2027 fills itself up.

1. Mark the fixed dates first (5 minutes)

Start with the things you can't move: birthdays, anniversaries, public holidays where you live, school breaks if you have kids, and any work dates that are already set, like a year-end close or a conference.

Use one colour or one symbol for all of these. A small dot is enough. The point is to see where the year is already spoken for.

2. Block the big rocks (5 minutes)

Next, the few things you want to happen in 2027 that need a block of time: a holiday, a move, a course, a launch, a long project. Draw a line across the weeks you'd like each one to take.

Keep it to three to five items. If you write down twelve, the page stops being useful and becomes a wish list.

3. Look for collisions (3 minutes)

Now look at the page as a whole. Where do two lines overlap? Where does a big rock sit right on top of a busy run of fixed dates? Where are three heavy months in a row with no break?

Move what you can. It's much easier to shift a holiday by two weeks in October than in May.

4. Protect some empty weeks (2 minutes)

Pick two or three weeks spread across the year and leave them deliberately empty. No trips, no launches. These are your buffer for the things you can't predict yet. When something unexpected turns up in June, you'll have somewhere to put it.

5. Put it where you'll see it (5 minutes)

A year plan only works if you look at it. Print it and pin it above your desk, on the fridge or at the front of a notebook. Then set a recurring reminder for the first day of each quarter to look at the page for five minutes and adjust.

Copy the fixed dates and big rocks into your calendar app too, so the reminders actually fire.

A quick checklist

  • Fixed dates marked in one colour
  • Three to five big rocks blocked out
  • Overlaps found and moved
  • Two or three empty buffer weeks
  • Page printed and somewhere visible
  • Quarterly five-minute review in your calendar
