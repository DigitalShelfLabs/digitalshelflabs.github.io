What to Put in a Vacation Rental Welcome Folder: 7 Pages That Answer Most Guest Questions

A practical checklist for short-term rental hosts, from the Wi-Fi card to the check-out sign

DigitalShelf Labs · 15 October 2026

If you host a short-term rental, you already know the messages. "What's the Wi-Fi password?" "Where do we put the rubbish?" "What time do we need to leave?" "Is there a pharmacy nearby?"

None of them are hard to answer. But answering the same five questions for every booking, often late at night, adds up. A simple printed welcome folder answers most of them before guests think to ask, and it makes your place feel cared for.

Here are the seven pages worth including, what to write on each, and a few tips on making them easy to use.

1. A welcome sign

This is the first thing guests see, so keep it warm and short. Include:

  • Your names (or the property name), so guests know who they're dealing with.
  • Two or three sentences of welcome: where to find the folder, and how to reach you if something is missing.
  • Check-in and check-out times.
  • One contact method, with a note on when you reply.

Place it somewhere visible from the door: on the kitchen counter, a hallway table or in a small frame.

2. House rules

House rules are easier to follow when they read like a friendly note, not a contract. Aim for six to ten short rules, each one line, written positively where you can. Common topics:

  • Smoking and vaping.
  • Quiet hours, with exact times.
  • Parties, events and overnight visitors.
  • Pets.
  • Shoes inside, if it matters to you.
  • Locking doors and closing windows.
  • Reporting breakages ("so we can fix it for the next guest" lands better than "you will be charged").
  • Recycling and bins.

Keep the rules on paper consistent with what you've written in your listing. If your area has its own requirements for posted information, follow those first and check them yourself.

3. A Wi-Fi card

The single most asked question deserves its own card. Write the network name and password large and clear, and add one practical tip, such as where the router is and that restarting it usually fixes a weak signal.

Print two: one framed near the living area, one by the bed or desk. Avoid easily confused characters in the password if you can choose it (l, 1 and I; O and 0).

4. A check-out sign

Check-out is where small misunderstandings turn into awkward messages. A clear sign avoids most of them. Include the check-out time and a short numbered list of steps, ideally five or six:

  1. Load and start the dishwasher (or leave dishes in the sink, whatever you prefer).
  2. Put used towels in one place.
  3. Take rubbish and recycling out.
  4. Turn off lights, heating or AC and appliances.
  5. Close windows and lock the door.
  6. Leave keys in a specific spot.

Be realistic. A long cleaning list in a rental with a cleaning fee feels unfair to guests. Ask only for what actually helps your turnover.

5. A local tips sheet

This page turns your place from "a room" into "a stay". List a handful of your own favourites rather than everything nearby:

  • Coffee, breakfast and one or two places for dinner.
  • The nearest groceries and pharmacy.
  • A park or walk.
  • A rainy-day option.
  • How to get around: bus stop, taxi number, parking notes.
  • One hidden gem.

Add a short detail to each ("order the cardamom bun", "5 minutes on foot"). That's what makes guests trust the list.

6. A "good to know" sheet

This is the practical page guests hope they won't need. Include the local emergency number, your phone number, the nearest hospital, where the fuse box and main water valve are, and where to find the first-aid kit and fire extinguisher. Add bin collection days if guests stay long enough to need them.

Keep it factual and calm. Its job is to save a guest from searching in a stressful moment.

7. A review-request card

Reviews matter to hosts, but guests rarely think about them unprompted. A small card in the welcome folder or by the door is a polite reminder. One or two sentences is enough: if they enjoyed the stay, a short review on the platform they booked with helps other travellers find you. Thank them, sign it, done.

Making the folder easy to use

  • One style for every page. Matching pages look intentional and are easier to scan.
  • Big, plain text. Guests read these on arrival, often tired and in a hurry.
  • Editable files. You will change details: a new Wi-Fi password, a café that closed. Keep the pages in a format you can update in minutes.
  • Print at 100%. Scaling to fit can cut off edges. Heavier paper for signs and cards feels nicer and lasts longer.
  • Review twice a year. Walk through the folder as if you were a guest, and fix anything outdated.
