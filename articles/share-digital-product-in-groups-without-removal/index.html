How to Share Your Digital Product in Online Groups Without Getting Removed

A 10-minute rules check, three verdicts, and a test for what to give away

DigitalShelf Labs · 11 October 2026

You made a PDF, a template, or a small toolkit. It's live. Nobody knows it exists.

The people who need it are already asking for help in Reddit communities, Facebook groups, Discord servers, and forums. So you post your link there, and one of three things happens: it's ignored, it's removed, or you get a warning.

Most of those removals aren't about a bad post. They're about a post in the wrong place, in the wrong thread, or from an account the group's filter doesn't trust yet. That part is fixable before you ever hit Post.

Here's the check I run on every group first.

Step 1: Find where the rules actually live

Rules are rarely in one place. Look in all of these:

  • Reddit: the About tab or sidebar, the community wiki, and pinned posts at the top of the feed.
  • Facebook groups: the About section, the rules list, and any pinned admin announcement.
  • Discord: the #rules or #welcome channel, plus any channel with "promo," "showcase," or "self-promo" in the name.
  • Forums, Slack, Circle: the guidelines or FAQ page, the welcome post, and the pinned post in each category.

Search what you find for these words: promo, self-promotion, links, spam, sell.

Then read the ten most recent posts and the ones with the most replies. The rules tell you what's allowed. The feed tells you what the group actually likes.

Step 2: Answer ten questions per group

Write these down once per group, with the date you checked:

  1. Is self-promotion allowed? Yes, no, or only in a specific thread?
  2. Is there a promo thread, day, or channel?
  3. Are links allowed in posts? In comments?
  4. Is there a minimum account age, karma, or join date?
  5. Do they ask you to say that you made it?
  6. Is there a required tag, flair, or post format?
  7. What do this month's top posts look like: questions, tutorials, showcases, stories?
  8. Has anyone shared their own product here and been treated well?
  9. Are the people here actually your buyer? What did they ask this week that your file answers?
  10. Who are the moderators?

Step 3: Give the group one verdict

Green. Promo is allowed where you plan to post, or there's a promo thread, and your account meets the rules. Post, follow their format exactly, say plainly that you made it, and use one link at most.

Yellow. The rules are silent, unclear, or say "ask first." Message a moderator, tell them what you'd like to share and where, and wait for a yes before you post anything.

Red. Promo is banned, or your account is too new for their filters. Don't mention your product at all. You can still answer people's questions well. That builds the account history some groups require, and it shows you what people really ask.

And one override: if question 9 is a no, skip the group, whatever the verdict. A Green group full of people who'll never buy is still a waste of your evening.

One group, one verdict. Write it down so you don't re-check every time you post.

Step 4: Decide what to give away

Even in a Green group, the post shouldn't be an ad. It should be one useful piece of your product, given away whole. People who want the rest will ask, or click your profile.

Before you use a piece, run three questions. You want three yeses:

  1. Can someone use this today without the rest of the file?
  2. Would I post this even if I had nothing to sell?
  3. Does it answer something people in this group asked in the last month?

How much to give matters. A checklist with three of fifteen items and "buy for the rest" reads as an ad. Five full items, each with a reason, reads as help. Keep the structure, the full set, and the worked-out templates in the paid file.

What this won't do

It won't guarantee views or sales, and it won't stop every removal. Every group sets its own rules and changes them, so read the current ones yourself each time. What it does is stop the avoidable removals: the wrong thread, the missing disclosure, the link where links aren't allowed.
