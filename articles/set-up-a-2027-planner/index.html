How to Set Up a 2027 Planner You'll Still Use in March

Goals, months, weeks, habits and reviews: a simple system that works on paper or on a tablet

DigitalShelf Labs · 17 October 2026

Every December, lots of people buy a new planner. By March, plenty of them are back in a drawer. The planner usually isn't the problem. The setup is: too many goals, too many pages to fill and no way to recover after a bad week.

Here is a simple system for 2027 that avoids those traps. It has five layers, each with a clear job, and it works the same way on paper or in a hyperlinked PDF on a tablet.

Layer 1: The year (one page, ten minutes)

Start with a year overview. Don't plan anything yet. Just mark the dates you already know: holidays you celebrate, birthdays, trips, deadlines, school terms.

This page is your early warning system. A glance tells you that March is packed or that August has a two-week gap. Many planners leave holidays blank on purpose, so they work in any country. Take the time to fill in yours.

Layer 2: A few goals, written properly

Pick no more than six goals for the year. Three is often better. For each, write:

  • The goal, as specific as you can. "Run a 10K" rather than "get fit".
  • Why it matters. This line is what you'll read in May when motivation dips.
  • By when. A month is enough.
  • First steps. Two or three actions you could take this month.

Goals without first steps tend to stay on the page. Goals with a first step that takes under an hour tend to start.

Layer 3: Monthly goals and a monthly spread

At the start of each month, look at your yearly goals and choose a top 3 for the month. These should be steps toward the yearly goals, not new ideas.

Then use the monthly calendar for appointments and deadlines, and a notes area for priorities. If a month is packed, it's fine for one of your top 3 to be "keep the habit going" rather than a big push.

Layer 4: Weekly pages, where the work happens

The weekly page is where your plans meet reality. Once a week, at a fixed time, spend ten minutes on it:

  1. Look at your monthly top 3.
  2. Pick a top 3 for this week.
  3. Spread tasks across the seven days, leaving space.
  4. Use notes for anything that doesn't have a day yet.

If you only keep one habit from this whole post, keep the weekly planning session. It's the hinge that connects goals to Tuesday afternoon.

Choose a start day that matches how you think. If your week starts on Sunday, a Monday-start layout creates small friction every week, and friction is what kills planners.

Layer 5: Habits and reviews

Habit trackers turn goals into daily behaviour. Track a handful of habits at most, ideally ones linked to your goals. Ten is a ceiling, not a target. A grid with one box per day makes streaks and gaps easy to see.

Monthly reviews are where you learn. At month end, spend ten minutes answering four questions:

  • How did this month feel, on a scale of 1 to 5?
  • What were my wins?
  • What did I learn?
  • What's next?

Add a quarterly review after March, June, September and December. This is where you're allowed to change a yearly goal. Dropping a goal on purpose is a decision. Letting it fade is just guilt.

Paper or tablet?

Both work. The difference is practical:

  • Printable planners are good if you like writing on paper, want to print only the month you're in, or prefer a planner on the desk. Check the paper size (A4, A5 or US Letter) and print at 100%.
  • Hyperlinked digital planners are good if you carry a tablet and stylus everywhere. Tabs and links let you jump from the year to a month to a week in one tap. In most PDF annotation apps, links work in read or view mode, and you write in pen mode.

Some people use both: a tablet planner for the week, and a printed goals and habits sheet on the wall.

Recovering from a bad week

You will miss a week. Plan for it now. When it happens, don't go back and fill in the old pages. Open the current week, set a top 3 and continue. If a whole month slipped, do a quick monthly review, then set up the current month. Five minutes, and you're back.

The whole system in one line

Year page once, goals once, a top 3 every month and every week, a small habit tracker, and a short review at the end of each month and quarter.
